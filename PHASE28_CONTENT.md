## Lesson 1: What is an ETF?
PAGE 1:
Imagine you want to eat at every restaurant in New York City. You could buy a food tour package that takes you to 500 at once. That package is an ETF. An Exchange-Traded Fund is a basket of investments bundled into one product.

PAGE 2:
SPY tracks the S&P 500. One share gives you Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and 495 others. Before ETFs in the 1990s, this diversification required buying hundreds of stocks individually.

PAGE 3:
Three advantages: instant diversification, low cost (0.03-0.20% vs 1-2% for managed funds), and transparency. A 1% fee difference costs hundreds of thousands over 30 years.

PAGE 4:
VTI holds 3,500 US companies. XLK holds only tech. AGG holds bonds. VXUS holds international. My Frontier combines types to capture growth from different economic areas.

PAGE 5:
Expense ratio is the annual fee. VTI charges 0.03% or  per 0,000. At 8% for 30 years: 00,627 with 0.03% fee vs 6,122 with 1.0% fee. You lose 4,505 to fees. Not financial advice.

## Lesson 2: What is Risk Tolerance?
PAGE 1:
Some people love roller coasters. Others prefer carousels. Risk tolerance describes how much volatility you can handle without making poor decisions like panic selling.

PAGE 2:
Emotional tolerance: how you feel when your portfolio drops 20%. Financial tolerance: can you afford a 20% drop? Young investors have high financial tolerance because time heals.

PAGE 3:
Conservative: 60-80% bonds, 4-6% returns. Moderate: 50-70% stocks, 6-9% returns. Aggressive: 80-100% stocks, highest long-term returns but 40-50% drops possible.

PAGE 4:
The critical mistake: overestimating emotional tolerance. In bull markets everyone thinks they are aggressive. When markets drop 30%, many sell at the worst time.

PAGE 5:
My Frontier derives risk from your sector selections. Tech and crypto = aggressive. Bonds and dividends = conservative. If a 35% drop would make you sell, choose more conservative. Not financial advice.

## Lesson 3: What is Diversification?
PAGE 1:
Dutch merchants in the 1600s spread cargo across many ships so one sinking would not ruin them. Diversification means owning assets that do not all fail at the same time.

PAGE 2:
When Company A rises and Company B falls, combining them reduces swings without reducing average return. Correlation measures this: 1.0 = lockstep, -1.0 = opposite, 0 = independent.

PAGE 3:
US stocks and bonds have low correlation. When stocks crash, investors flee to bonds. A 70/30 stock-bond portfolio loses less in crashes than 100% stocks.

PAGE 4:
During severe crises, correlations spike. But damage is always less diversified. A 55% stock drop needs 122% gain to recover. A 25% drop needs only 35%.

PAGE 5:
My Frontier scores diversification 0-10. Score 7+ means well-diversified. Below 5 means too concentrated. Add uncorrelated sectors to improve. Not financial advice.
